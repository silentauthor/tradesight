# Stock/Options/Swing/Forex — Matthew Bear, Ray Bears, "Ambush," Tony Correra

## Swing trading rules (Ray Bears)

- Definition: impulsive/directional moves lasting roughly two weeks to two months; hold at minimum overnight. Swing traders care about price swings, not the underlying company; trade both directions (long and short/puts).
- A support/resistance level is only valid once price has touched it at least twice. Buy near support, exit before resistance rather than waiting for the exact touch.
- Daily chart is the shortest timeframe for swing trading — micro timeframes generate false triggers.
- Use breakout strategy in an uptrend, breakdown strategy in a downtrend; only trade stocks with a clear trend, skip sideways/indecisive charts.
- Fix stop AND target at order entry, never adjust after; check the trade daily to move the stop toward breakeven.
- Stock selection: high liquidity/volume, moderate volatility (avoid >2% daily whipsaw), good fundamentals, heavy institutional ownership, and a tendency to track its sector. No penny stocks; avoid names destabilized by fresh news/earnings/litigation.
- Trailing-stop example: buy at $80 targeting $100 with an initial stop at $70; a 5% trailing stop raises to $95 once price hits $100, and to $114 once price hits $120.
- Day-trade routine: fixed risk:reward of 1:2 or 1:3 (never higher), stop and target set at order entry and left alone as a beginner; avoid stocks moving more than 2% intraday (too volatile to predict); cover any open position by the final session of the day regardless of outcome.
- Options-specific liquidity screen: only trade options with open interest at least 40x the number of contracts you want to trade.

## Options concepts (Matthew Bear)

- Before any options trade: check expected price change before expiration, volatility of the underlying, and time remaining to profit.
- Delta near 0.5 = at-the-money; avoid options with delta around 0.4/-0.4 (statistically unlikely to be profitable by expiry).
- Gamma rises sharply near expiration — big gamma means big potential profit and big potential risk.
- Theta guarantees continuous value loss for the holder; a position stays net-favorable to the holder only while delta-driven gains outpace theta decay.
- Cardinal rule: never invest more in a single trade than you can afford to lose.
- Options are appropriate only when you have a specific market outlook, a matched risk profile, and you genuinely understand the strategy — "if you do not understand a strategy initially, do not attempt it." Prefer spreads over naked single-leg positions to cut cost and risk.

## Options exit rules (Ray Bears)

- Exit a losing options trade at a 50% loss of the premium paid.
- Take profit once gains exceed 75% of the premium paid (or received, for a short option); buy back a short option once 80% of the initial premium has been captured.

## Risk management (Ray Bears)

- Never put more than 2% of total capital into a single trade. Keep a separate "risk account" buffer of roughly 5% of total capital.
- Diversification rule of thumb: never more than 10% of capital in one type of stock.
- Forex-specific: risk 1–2% of capital per trade, expressed as a fixed cash amount; position size = (max cash risk) ÷ (stop distance in pips) ÷ (lot value), then divided further if scaling in across multiple orders.

## The "Ambush" strategy (forex stop-order trap)

Core principle: price only moves by making higher highs or lower lows; the shadow/wick is "virtual," the close is what's "real." No indicators used — clean chart, candlesticks only.

- **Basic setup**: at the day's open, place a buy-stop at the previous candle's high and a sell-stop at the previous candle's low simultaneously (a two-sided trap). Whichever order fills, close the position at that same period's close.
- **Refinement**: if price has already broken one side of the previous bar before you place orders, only place the stop order on the *opposite*, not-yet-broken side — this meaningfully raises the win rate versus the naive two-sided version.
- **Multiple positions**: rather than one large trade, stack successive same-direction stop orders bar after bar and close them together — an early loser can be offset by later winners in the same batch.
- Losses occur when the stop fills but the bar closes back inside the prior range; this is accepted and the trade closed at that bar's close (a time-based exit, not a technical stop-loss).

## Stock selection / screening criteria (Ray Bears)

- No penny stocks; require high liquidity/volume and moderate volatility.
- Prefer "correlative" stocks that track their sector/index in both bull and bear conditions over "vagabond" stocks with no readable pattern.
- Favor heavy institutional ownership (mutual/hedge funds) — less prone to panic dumping.
- Specific screens: stocks testing a known support/resistance level, stocks near 52-week highs/lows, weekly top gainers/losers, and stocks with a fresh news catalyst (for a quick in-and-out trade only).

## Correra — indicator-based swing systems (specific parameters)

- CCI + moving average: 7-day and 14-day EMAs; after a retracement, price returning to the MA with CCI ≤ 100 = buy signal, CCI > 100 = sell signal.
- Floor Trader method: 9-period EMA crossing an 18-period EMA signals the trend; enter on a retracement that touches the 9-EMA.
- Bollinger Bands: 20-period MA, 2 standard deviations; a candle touching an upward-sloping band = buy, a downward-sloping band = sell.
- ADX strategy: 20-period EMA crossing above a 40-period EMA plus ADX ≥ 30 (a strong trend) = buy, with the stop at the 40-period MA.
- Supertrend: period 7, multiplier 3 — green (below price) = buy signal, turning red = sell signal.
- Hull moving averages: 7- and 14-day; the short crossing above the long = buy.
- Account guidance: start around $5,000 (even $500–1,000 to learn); wait 3–6 months of cash-account experience before using margin.
