# Candlesticks Explained Visually — Keith J. Adams

Short, slide-deck-style book. Most content is standard candlestick pattern definitions (Marubozu, Spinning Top, the 4 dojis, Engulfing, Dark Cloud, Piercing, Harami, Shooting Star, Doji Star, Three Methods, Hammer) already covered by the other candlestick sources. Notes below are the material that goes beyond generic textbook coverage.

## Volume-gating rule (non-standard, binary filter)

After recognizing any pattern, check volume against a rolling comparison chart. This author's decision table: **Low Volume → No Trade; Medium Volume → No Trade; High Volume → Trade.** Most texts treat "above average volume" as sufficient; this author explicitly excludes medium volume too — only outlier/standout volume spikes qualify a pattern for action.

## Doji sub-type ranking

- Dragonfly Doji → reliable bullish reversal signal (top or bottom).
- Gravestone Doji → reliable bearish reversal signal.
- Long-Legged Doji → pure indecision, no directional edge.
- Standard Doji → "does not signify anything, check previous trend" — explicitly downgraded versus the other three.

## Engulfing pattern — confirmation rules

- Stronger signal when: the first day's body is very small (near-doji) and the second day's body is very long (the bigger the size disparity, the stronger); heavy volume on the engulfing body; the engulfing body covers more than one prior real body.
- Reversal requires third-day confirmation: a same-direction candle, a gap in the new direction, or a higher/lower close.
- **Context rule**: Bullish Engulfing after a bearish trend = tradeable; Bullish Engulfing after a bullish trend (continuation context) = "Risky," explicitly labeled no-trade. Same logic mirrored for Bearish Engulfing.

## Dark Cloud Cover / Piercing Pattern — precise gap requirement

- Dark Cloud: the second (red) candle must **gap above the high** of the first green candle (a true gap, not just a lower close), then close within/below the first candle's midpoint.
- Piercing: the second (green) candle must **gap below the low** of the prior red candle, close above its midpoint, but fail to close above its open.

## Neck patterns (On Neck / In Neck / Thrusting)

All occur in a downtrend; the second candle gaps below the low of the first long red candle (same setup as Piercing) but are framed as **bearish continuation**, not bullish reversal. Core rule: the midpoint of the first candle acts as resistance — any second-day recovery candle that fails to close above that 50% level is bearish regardless of how constructive it looks. Trigger: a move below the low of any of these three patterns signals further weakness ahead — trade the breakdown below the pattern's low, not the pattern itself.

## Harami — relative strength

Ranked as a weaker reversal signal than a Hammer or Engulfing Pattern — "momentum abating" rather than a strong reversal. The Harami Cross (baby candle = a doji) is the most powerful variant.

## Shooting Star — consolidation-breakout trigger

Both bullish and bearish versions are framed as a trend run into a small-bodied candle, followed by a small sideways consolidation box, with the actual signal being a candle that gaps *out* of that box.

## Doji Star — 5-candle setup

Stricter than the typical 3-candle Morning/Evening Star: requires three prior same-direction candles, then the doji, then one confirming opposite-direction candle.

## When NOT to use candlesticks

Sideways/range-bound market; no/low volume; a history of the signal failing repeatedly at this level; signal not aligning with pending/floating news.

## Personal trading setups

- Positional trades: daily candlesticks + volume + option-chain analysis.
- Day trades: 30-minute candlesticks + volume + a dual-period RSI(7)/RSI(12) pairing (an uncommon oscillator combination not found in standard candlestick references).
