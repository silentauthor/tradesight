# The Candlestick Trading Bible — Sanyog Raut

Core recurring framework: **Trend → Level → Signal**. Identify market direction, find the most important key level (support/resistance, supply/demand zone, moving average, trendline, Fibonacci retracement), then wait for a candlestick signal to form there in line with the trend before entering. A pattern alone is not tradeable — one or two confluence factors combined with a clear signal is enough for a high-probability trade.

## Patterns

- **Bearish/Bullish Engulfing Bar**: two candles, first body smaller, second (opposite color) body fully engulfs it. Nison's 3 validity criteria: market must be in a clear trend, engulfing candle = 2 candlesticks with the first body fully engulfed, second body is the opposite color. Bullish version is more powerful as a reversal at the end of a downtrend ("capitulation bottom") than as a mid-uptrend continuation signal.
- **Doji**: open ≈ close; indicates indecision. At top/bottom of a trend = reversal signal.
- **Dragonfly Doji**: bullish; open/high/close all ≈ equal, long lower tail — buyer rejection, most reliable near support.
- **Gravestone Doji**: bearish mirror — seller rejection, most reliable near resistance.
- **Morning Star**: 3 candles — bearish candle, small indecisive body, bullish candle gapping up and closing above the midpoint of candle 1. Most powerful near support at the bottom of a downtrend.
- **Evening Star**: mirror topping pattern.
- **Hammer** (bullish pin bar): open ≈ high ≈ close, long lower shadow. Reversal at bottom of a downtrend.
- **Shooting Star** (bearish pin bar): open ≈ low ≈ close, long upper shadow — shadow should be roughly twice the real body. Most reliable near resistance.
- **Harami/Inside Bar**: large "mother" candle then a smaller "baby" candle whose range closes inside the mother's range — consolidation/indecision. At a trend extreme = reversal; mid-trend = continuation. Per Bulkowski (cited in this book): bearish inside bar in a bull market → bearish reversal ~65% of the time; bullish continuation ~52%; bullish "abandoned baby" variant → bullish reversal 70% (bull markets) / 55% (bear markets).
- **Tweezers Top/Bottom**: two candles closing back near the first candle's open at a trend extreme.
- **Pin Bar** (general treatment): long tail/rejection wick with a small real body near one end. Bullish-colored bullish pin bars and bearish-colored bearish pin bars are more powerful. Quality criteria: form on 4H/daily+ timeframes only, in-trend is more powerful than counter-trend, longer tail = more powerful, must occur at a major key level.

## Strategy chapters

**Pin Bar strategy**: trend + key level (S/R or the 21-period MA) + a pin bar forming in line with the trend at that level. Aggressive entry = immediately after the pin bar closes; conservative entry = after a 50% retracement into the pin bar's range (better R:R, sometimes misses the trade). Stop just beyond the pin bar's tail. Target = next major S/R level. Confluence tools: 8 & 21 MAs, 50%/61.8% Fibonacci retracement, trendlines, Bollinger Bands (a false breakout of the band plus a horizontal level = confirmation in range-bound markets).

**Engulfing Bar strategy**: same trend/level/signal framework. Works with 8 & 21 SMA as dynamic S/R (don't use moving averages in range-bound/untradeable markets — false signals). Supply/demand zone quality: strength of the departure move, adequate risk/reward, use bigger timeframes for zone identification. Minimum acceptable reward:risk before taking a signal: **2:1**.

**Inside Bar strategy**: in trending markets, wait for the inside bar to form in-trend, enter on a breakout of its range; stop beyond the mother candle; target = next S/R level. Trade bigger timeframes (daily/4H) — lower timeframes cause overtrading and false signals.

**Inside Bar False Breakout** (a stop-hunt pattern): price breaks out of the inside bar/mother-candle range then quickly reverses back inside — explicit evidence of stop-loss hunting by institutions who drive price to levels with clustered stops before reversing. Trade the fade of the false breakout; stop at/just beyond the hunted extreme — explicitly do NOT place your own stop exactly at the obvious horizontal level, since that's where it gets hunted.

## Market structure

- Three market types: trending, ranging, choppy. Trends occur roughly **30% of the time** (this book's stated statistic — corroborated independently by Edwards & Magee and by Wilder's ADX convention cited in Murphy).
- Determine market structure/trend only on bigger timeframes (4H, daily, weekly) — never on smaller timeframes.
- Ranging markets: confirm with at least 2 touches of support AND 2 touches of resistance; range boundaries are frequently overshot to trap traders before reversing.
- Best entries are at the start of a fresh impulsive move (after a pullback completes at a level), not mid-pullback.
- Top-down/multi-timeframe analysis: always check a higher timeframe before entering — a valid daily signal can fail outright into an unchecked weekly resistance level (a real case study is given where this happened).

## Risk management

- Minimum reward:risk before taking any signal: **2:1**. Worked math: 10 trades at 3:1 R:R risking $200 each, even losing 7 of 10, still nets a profit ($1,800 won vs $1,400 lost) — illustrates that win-rate alone doesn't determine profitability.
- Risk per trade: professionals cap at **2%** of equity; beginners should cap at **1%**.
- Always use a real stop-loss order on the platform, never a mental stop.
- Never risk money you can't afford to lose; never borrow to trade; grow the account slowly.
