# The Art of Trading — Trader Stewie

## Position sizing & risk rules (the book's baseline account model: $100k, scales to any size)

- **Rule 1**: never lose more than 1% of total account capital per trade. Aggressive traders might stretch to 1.5–2%; the author can't think of a good reason to go higher.
- **Rule 2**: size positions to stay unemotional — break the account into 10% increments, enter almost every position with ~10% of capital.
- **Rule 3**: only 5–6 open positions at a time. "Less is more."
- **Rule 4**: stop watching account value; let stops do the work. The urge to micromanage means the position is too big.
- **Rule 5**: think in percentages, not dollars — 5% of $10,000 is $500 whether that's 10 shares of a $1,000 stock or 1,000 shares of a $10 stock.
- Exceptions: 3x leveraged ETFs and very volatile names don't follow the standard rules — size down.
- In turbulent markets: cut losers fast, add to winners only when they're already showing strength, never add to a loser, reduce open positions to 3–4 (or fewer), prefer index ETFs, consider VXX hedges.
- Never hold a position overnight through its earnings report.

## Chart patterns (with entry/stop/target rules)

- **Bull/Bear Flag**: sharp "flagpole" move + a parallel-channel consolidation drifting against the pole. Entry on breakout of the channel; stop beyond the opposite trendline; target = flagpole length projected from the breakout.
- **Bullish/Bearish Pennant**: same as a flag but the consolidation converges (a small symmetrical triangle) rather than running parallel. The tighter the price action at the apex, the quicker it explodes out.
- **Falling Wedge** (bullish continuation, only in an uptrend — never trade it in a downtrend): converging down-sloping trendlines during a pullback in an established uptrend. Entry on break of the upper trendline; stop below the wedge low; target = pattern's widest height projected from the breakout. Walk the stop up as the trade works.
- **Symmetrical Triangle** (continuation/neutral): converging trendlines; buy only on a confirmed upside breakout ("from contraction comes expansion").
- **Inverted Head & Shoulders** (bullish reversal): three swing lows, middle one lowest; entry on neckline break; stop below the right shoulder (short-term) or below the head (longer-term); target = head-to-neckline distance projected up from the neckline.
- **Cup and Handle**: handle depth should be 1/3–1/2 of the cup depth, handle length ≤ half the cup length; entry on confirmed neckline breakout; stop below the handle low; target = cup depth projected up from the neckline.
- **2B Bottom Reversal** (a stop-run/bear-trap pattern): price makes a low, bounces, breaks below that low (a bear trap), then on volume clears the prior lower high. Buy as price reclaims the broken low; stop at the reversal (undercut) lows; target = at least half the initial drop, ideally the full retracement.
- **Bearish Power Earnings Gap (B-PEG)**: a stock in a downtrend reports weak earnings, gaps down hard, and — critically — closes near the session lows (the close matters as much as the gap). Add to a short watchlist; use technicals (bear flags, rising wedges) for the actual entry timing later.
- **Rising Wedge** (bearish, best in an already-established downtrend): a low-quality, low-volume dead-cat-bounce rally into converging trendlines. Entry on break of the lower trendline; stop above the wedge highs; target = measured move from the wedge's widest range.
- **Descending Triangle** (bearish continuation): flat horizontal support + descending upper trendline; breaks are usually fast and dramatic. Entry on support breakdown; stop above the last lower high; target = pattern height projected down.
- **Holy Grail setup** (trend-pullback buy, originally Linda Raschke): a strongly uptrending stock's first or second pullback to the 20-day EMA that holds/reclaims it. Must be a "smart pullback" — slow, steady, low-volume, multi-day. Do NOT buy the 3rd+ retest of the same MA — bounce odds decay fast.

## Candlestick-adjacent single-bar setups

- **Hammer**: lower shadow ≥ 2–3x the body; green (bullish close) is more bullish than red; requires the next candle to trade above and hold above the hammer's body before it's tradeable.
- **Inside Days**: current day's range fully inside the prior day's range — never trade standalone, only as a coil confirming a nearby quality setup (e.g. a bull flag near the top of its range about to break out).

## Market-timing tools

- **NYMO (NYSE McClellan Oscillator)**: −60 = entering oversold; −80 or lower = extreme fear (once/twice a year); −100 or lower = extremely rare panic. Strongest buy signal: a positive divergence where a second selloff makes a new price low but NYMO makes a higher low than the first selloff. In bear markets, flip the read — a NYMO push into overbought during a bear-market bounce is a signal to add shorts, not to buy the dip.
- **RSI + MACD divergence**: price retesting a prior low/support while RSI and MACD both print higher readings than at the first test = the second decline is weak — a strong reversal signal, especially on index charts.
- **Double Bottom Divergence (DBD) day trade**: an actual low prints, a weak bounce fails, then a retest of the low (or a slight undercut) coincides with RSI/MACD making higher lows than the first low — buy the retest with a stop below the double-bottom structure.
- **Volume/distribution reading**: heavy-volume down days closing near the lows followed by much lighter-volume weak bounces = institutional distribution, a warning sign near market tops, not a buy-the-dip signal. The reverse (heavy-volume down days with rotational/controlled selling that never lets breadth oscillators reach oversold) is described as a "controlled selling" warning of a further, prolonged decline.
- Bear markets: selling is orderly and relentless, not panicky. Watch for low-volume short-covering pops in premarket futures after multi-day beatings — these rarely last more than a day.

## Discipline & psychology rules

- In choppy markets, most breakouts fail — trade pullbacks-to-support at confluence levels instead, and book gains quickly since trends die fast in chop.
- Don't chase a missed breakout — wait for the low-volume retest of the breakout area instead.
- The news is almost always irrelevant; only the market's reaction to news matters.
- There's a time to just sit and do nothing — if unsure of the market, sit out.
- Always come into a trade with a plan: reason, setup, entry/exit points, size, and capital at risk if stopped.
- Test a new strategy over at least 20 mechanical trades sized small enough to survive losing all of them.
