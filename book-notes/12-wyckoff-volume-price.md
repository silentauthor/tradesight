# Wyckoff 2.0 (Rubén Villahermosa) & A Complete Guide to Volume Price Analysis (Anna Coulling)

## Wyckoff accumulation schematic (range-bound smart-money buying)

- **Phase A**: Preliminary Support (first notable volume/spread-narrowing after a decline) → Selling Climax (panic, high-volume, wide-range down bar) → Automatic Rally (a sharp bounce on decreasing volume, setting the range's top) → Secondary Test (a retest of the climax low on visibly lower volume/narrower spread).
- **Phase B**: range-bound, volume generally contracting as the "cause" builds; may include a deceptive test near the range low.
- **Phase C**: a **Spring** (or deeper "Terminal Shakeout") — price pushes below the range's established support, ideally on volume equal to or lower than the original selling climax (showing absorption, not fresh selling), then swiftly reclaims the level. A low-volume test of the spring low follows.
- **Phase D**: Sign of Strength — a wide-range up bar closing near its high on distinctly increased volume, breaking internal range resistance — followed by a Last Point of Support (a pullback on light volume that holds well above the spring low).
- **Phase E**: markup, with recurring Sign-of-Strength bars on expanding volume and low-volume pullbacks to the breakout level.

Distribution is the mirror structure (Buying Climax, Upthrust/UTAD in place of Spring, Sign of Weakness, Last Point of Supply) — one key asymmetry: volume tends to *contract* through accumulation's Phase B but stay *elevated* through distribution's Phase B, since supply is actively being sold into strength.

## Springs and upthrusts — the key discriminator vs. a generic liquidity sweep

A Wyckoff spring/upthrust requires more than a wick beyond a prior swing point: (1) it must occur within an already-**matured** trading range (real cause built in Phase A/B, not just any swing high/low), and (2) the breach bar's volume should be **equal to or lower** than the volume at the range's earlier extremes — a breach on new or expanding volume is more likely a genuine breakout/breakdown, not a shakeout. Trading rule: don't enter on the spring/upthrust bar itself — wait for the reclaim, then either a Sign-of-Strength/Weakness break of internal structure, or a low-volume pullback test (the Last Point of Support/Supply), which is the actual low-risk entry.

## Volume Profile

**Point of Control (POC)**: the price level with the single highest traded volume over a lookback window — computed by binning volume by price. **Value Area**: the price band holding a target share (conventionally 70%) of total volume, built outward from the POC by repeatedly adding whichever adjacent bin has more volume. **High/Low Volume Nodes**: local maxima (act as magnets/support-resistance, price tends to slow there) and minima (thin "acceptance," price tends to move through quickly) in the volume-at-price histogram. A rising sequence of POCs across periods = accepted higher value = bullish continuation; a POC that fails to migrate higher after a rally, or migrates down, signals rejection. A "naked" POC (never revisited since) acts as a magnet/high-probability target.

## Volume-price analysis rules (Coulling)

Systematic table linking price direction to volume: rising price + rising volume = valid/bullish; rising price + falling volume = an anomaly, a rally lacking real conviction, likely to stall; falling price + rising volume = valid/bearish; falling price + falling volume = an anomaly, a decline lacking real selling pressure, likely to stall. Ultra-high volume on a narrow-range bar with little price progress signals hidden absorption (large buying or selling being masked) and often precedes a reversal. A genuine breakout from congestion should show rising volume above the range's average; a breakout on average/low volume is unreliable. A pullback/test after a genuine breakout should occur on **low** volume — elevated volume on the pullback is a warning the breakout may fail.

- **No-demand bar**: a narrow-range up-candle in an uptrend or at resistance, on below-average volume — buyers aren't committing, an exhaustion warning.
- **No-supply bar**: the mirror at support in a downtrend.
- **Volume at support/resistance retests**: a retest on markedly *lower* volume than the level's original formation signals absorption and a likely hold; a retest on equal or higher volume signals the level is likely to give way.
- **Climactic volume**: extreme volume with a reversal-shaped bar (long wick, or a narrow-spread bar failing to progress despite high volume) at a trend extreme marks a probable selling or buying climax; the actionable trigger is the subsequent low-volume successful test, not the climax bar itself.

## Cross-cutting principle from both sources

Volume-based signals should always be judged **relative to a rolling average and the volume at that specific price level's prior tests**, never against a fixed absolute threshold. The master heuristic is "effort vs. result": compare a bar's volume (effort) to its price displacement (result) — agreement (high effort/big result, or low effort/small result) is a valid move; disagreement is the anomaly worth flagging.
