# Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns (3rd ed.) — Thomas Bulkowski

Statistically backtested pattern reference (~1,396 stocks, 1991–2020, 150,000+ pattern instances). Every pattern below reports: breakeven failure rate (% that don't move even 5% past the breakout), average move for successful breakouts, and a performance rank (1 = best) out of the pool of patterns tested in that market condition. Note: numbers here are from the book's own "Summary of Statistics" tables, the most reliable section of the copy that was read.

## General principles

- **Confirmation is universal**: a pattern isn't valid until price *closes* beyond the confirmation line (neckline, prior swing extreme, trendline). Trading before confirmation is a major, quantified source of losses.
- **Breakeven failure rate** is the book's core reliability metric; failure rates escalate quickly at larger move thresholds (e.g. an H&S top failing to fall 5% happens rarely, but failing to fall 20%+ happens the majority of the time).
- **Bear-market moves complete roughly twice as fast** as equivalent bull-market moves.
- **Volume trend during formation is repeatedly said not to determine performance**, though the typical signature (declining volume through most consolidation patterns) is still useful for identification.
- **Measured-move price target** = pattern height added to (or subtracted from) the breakout price; hit rates vary enormously by pattern, from ~29% to ~82%.
- **Throwbacks/pullbacks** after a breakout occur roughly 60–67% of the time across most patterns — a typical, not distinguishing, feature.
- Several textbook-famous patterns (symmetrical triangles, rising wedges, ascending triangles) perform poorly in Bulkowski's actual statistics despite their popularity; cup-with-handle and rectangles are consistently strong performers.
- All statistics represent theoretically "perfect" trades (buying exactly at the breakout, selling exactly at the ultimate high/low) — meant for relative comparison between patterns, not as an expectation of achievable real-world return.
