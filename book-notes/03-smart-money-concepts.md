# Advanced Technical Analysis — Smart Money Concepts — Jon Fibonacci

Forex/futures-centric book (predates/parallels ICT terminology). Its "Institutional Candle" = order block; "liquidity"/stop-runs = liquidity sweeps; Fibonacci "expensive/meeting-in-the-middle/cheap" zones = premium/equilibrium/discount.

## Core thesis

Smart money = capital controlled by institutions (endowments, banks, mutual/hedge/pension funds, insurers) — they control price; retail doesn't. ~96% of traders lose money, so watch what retail does and take the opposite side. Textbook trendlines/flags/harmonics/classic breakouts are the exact patterns institutions exploit. Minimum reward:risk **1:3**, sometimes up to 1:10+.

## Liquidity / stop runs

Price gravitates to where untriggered orders rest — above equal/double/triple highs, below equal/double/triple lows. A "run" = a candle piercing below/above the equal level then closing back inside and reversing. Do NOT buy a retail double-bottom with a stop under the lows — that stop cluster is the target; instead enter where retail gets stopped out, riding the reversal.

## Institutional Candle (order block)

The last down candle (or series) immediately preceding an explosive up-move = where smart money was buying while price fell. Qualifying context: (1) a new high forms then price pulls back; (2) check how price *left* the origin — casual/normal-size candles = weak, violent/explosive departure = high demand; (3) zone = the candle's open-to-close body; on weekly/monthly candles, use the body's midpoint instead of the full range. Entry: limit order inside the body as price returns. Invalidation: price trades through the entire body to its close — cut the loss. Sizing rule given: risk ≈1% of account on this trade for potential 5%+ account gains at 1:5+ R:R.

## Correlation divergence

Two normally correlated assets fall out of sync — one makes a higher low while the other makes a lower low (or the mirror at highs). The stronger one (leader) is the buy candidate; the weaker one (laggard) is the short candidate. Strongest when it forms at a higher-timeframe support/resistance level. Example correlated sets: EUR/USD–GBP/USD, AUD/USD–NZD/USD, Gold–Silver, index futures, single stocks vs. their index.

## EUR/GBP triangulation (a specific trade-selection matrix)

Uses EUR/GBP cross direction + DXY (dollar index) direction + which pair shows the divergence to decide whether to trade EUR/USD or GBP/USD, and long or short — short the weak currency, not the strong one.

## Open interest / COT

Falling open interest = commercials releasing shorts = bullish; rising OI = more shorts = bearish. Used only as a higher-timeframe bias, always combined with correlation divergence, never alone.

## Round/psychological levels

Levels ending in 00/20/50/80 at the hundred-pip scale act as price magnets/reaction points, independent of volume history — strongest at new all-time highs.

## Fibonacci logic (this book's premium/discount)

Retracement drawn over the impulse leg: below 50% = still "expensive" (don't buy yet); 50% = "meeting in the middle"; above 50% = "cheap." Favorite levels: 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%. Buy retracements into the 61.8–78.6% zone in a bullish market (mirror for shorts); beyond 78.6% the move may be failing entirely.

## Setups (summary)

- **Institutional Candle pullback** — the core setup, described above.
- **Stop-run reversal at a double/triple bottom/top** — enter in the stop-hunt zone as retail gets shaken out.
- **Correlation-divergence reversal** — trade the leader/laggard divergence, ideally at a higher-timeframe level.
- **Fibonacci-cheap continuation** — buy a sharp shakeout dip into the 61.8–78.6% zone of an established uptrend for trend continuation.

## Risk management

Risk 1–2% of account per trade (author personally uses ~1%). Minimum reward:risk **1:3**, often much higher. Capital preservation is priority #1, risk management #2. Never widen a stop as price approaches it; a break-even trade beats a losing trade. No weekend holds (Sunday gap risk past the stop).

## Red flags

Buying after an extended rally with no deep retracement; a "casual" (non-explosive) departure from a candidate zone; price retracing through an Institutional Candle's full body; still above the 50% Fibonacci retracement ("too expensive"); retail-obvious patterns (clean double tops, trendlines, textbook breakouts) which get run for stops; rising open interest into a move; London session and London/NY overlap are when stop-hunt manipulation is most frequent.
